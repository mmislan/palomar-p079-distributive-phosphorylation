\author{Michael Mislan}

\begin{abstract}
The sequential distributive \(n\)-site phosphorylation cycle, in which one kinase and one
phosphatase act on a substrate with \(n\) ordered sites, is the standard mass-action model of
multisite protein modification. Wang and Sontag proved that it has at most \(2n-1\) positive
equilibria in each compatibility class, and the best general multistability result, due to
Feliu, Rendall and Wiuf, provides \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) asymptotically stable equilibria. Both
capacities are determined exactly. For every \(n\ge1\), and for one common list of positive rate
constants and one compatibility class, the maximal number of positive equilibria is \(2n-1\)
and the maximal number of locally asymptotically stable equilibria is \(n\). The upper bound
\(n\) is proved by degree theory and parametric transversality and includes nonhyperbolic
attractors. The lower bounds are attained simultaneously by rational data: \(2n-1\) hyperbolic
equilibria, of which \(n\) are sinks and \(n-1\) are saddles with a one-dimensional unstable
manifold, and the same configuration persists on a nonempty open subset of the
\((6n+3)\)-dimensional space of rate constants and totals. The equilibria are produced by an
explicit positive polynomial recurrence together with an interlacing argument. Stability is
obtained by separating equilibrium geometry from kinetic time scales: after coalescing the
equilibria, fast binding reduces the dynamics to a slow system on substrate inventories that
retain the enzyme-bound substrate, and for a large kinase excess its transverse part converges
to a path matrix with a rank-one positive feedback whose gain is strictly less than one by an
explicit margin. Additional results include a diagonal Lyapunov certificate, the exact splitting law of the
critical eigenvalue, exact rational witnesses for \(n\le7\), and a three-site benchmark with a
rigorous local robustness and recovery certificate. The Lean~4 formalization covers the two
capacity ceilings, rational simultaneous sink/saddle attainment and open persistence, the
general prescribed-root construction and its coefficient and interlacing claims, the
quantitative splitting law and compact-current refinements, and the fixed spectra, benchmark
and operational recovery certificates. Supporting Lean proofs construct positive analytic
unstable curves at the same rational family's saddles, with backward trajectories tending
to their equilibria. The precise formal
statements and source-bound verification records are described in Section~\ref{sec:formal}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

\subsection{Background}
Reversible phosphorylation at several sites is one of the most common mechanisms by which cells
regulate protein function \cite{Cohen2000,SalazarHofer2009}. In the simplest mechanistic
description a single kinase \(E\) adds phosphate groups to a substrate one site at a time in a
fixed order, a single phosphatase \(F\) removes them in the reverse order, and each enzyme
releases the substrate after every catalytic event. This is the \emph{sequential distributive}
mechanism, or multiple futile cycle. Under mass-action kinetics it is the reaction network
\begin{equation}\label{eq:network}
\begin{aligned}
 S_{i-1}+E &\ \underset{b_i}{\overset{a_i}{\rightleftharpoons}}\ C_i
       \ \xrightarrow{\ c_i\ }\ S_i+E,\\[3pt]
 S_i+F &\ \underset{\beta_i}{\overset{\alpha_i}{\rightleftharpoons}}\ Y_i
       \ \xrightarrow{\ \gamma_i\ }\ S_{i-1}+F,
       \qquad i=1,\dots,n,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where \(S_i\) is the substrate with \(i\) phosphorylated sites, \(C_i\) and \(Y_i\) are the
kinase--substrate and phosphatase--substrate complexes, and all \(6n\) rate constants are
strictly positive. There are \(3n+3\) species and three conserved totals,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:totals}
 \ET=E+\sum_{i=1}^n C_i,\qquad
 \FT=F+\sum_{i=1}^n Y_i,\qquad
 \ST=\sum_{i=0}^n S_i+\sum_{i=1}^n(C_i+Y_i).
\end{equation}
The set of nonnegative concentrations with prescribed positive totals is a
\emph{compatibility class}; it is invariant under the dynamics and has dimension \(3n\).
Throughout this paper every statement about stability refers to the dynamics \emph{within} a
compatibility class, that is, with the three totals fixed.

Markevich, Hoek and Kholodenko observed that the two-site cycle can be bistable although it has
no feedback other than competition for the shared enzymes \cite{Markevich2004}. This started a
long line of work on the equilibria of \eqref{eq:network}; see
\cite{Gunawardena2005,ConradiFlockerziRaisch2008,ThomsonGunawardena2009,FeliuWiuf2012,
HolsteinFlockerziConradi2013,ConradiMincheva2014,HellRendall2015,CFMW2017,
BihanDickensteinGiaroli2020,FKdWY2022,TorresFeliu2021} and the surveys
\cite{ConradiShiu2018,Dickenstein2016,Dickenstein2020}. The network has become a standard test
case for algebraic and analytic methods in reaction network theory: it is large, biologically
meaningful, and its steady-state variety has a monomial parametrization \cite{PDSC2012}.

Three results frame the present work. Wang and Sontag \cite{WangSontag2008} proved that for all
rate constants and all totals there are at most \(2n-1\) positive equilibria; they also
exhibited \(n+1\) (\(n\) even) or \(n\) (\(n\) odd) equilibria and conjectured that \(n+1\) is
never exceeded. Flockerzi, Holstein and Conradi \cite{FHC2014} refuted that conjecture by
constructing five equilibria for \(n=3\) and seven for \(n=4\), so that the ceiling \(2n-1\) is
attained for \(n\le4\); their method requires the solution of a system of polynomial
inequalities that grows with \(n\). Whether \(2n-1\) is attained for larger \(n\) has been
recorded as open \cite{FRW2020,FKdWY2023}. Feliu, Rendall and Wiuf \cite{FRW2020} proved
``unlimited multistability'': for every \(n\) there are parameters with
\(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) equilibria of which \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) are asymptotically
stable. Their proof coalesces equilibria in a Michaelis--Menten limit and uses a
centre-manifold and singular-perturbation argument, and they explain why that limit cannot
produce \(2n-1\) equilibria when \(n\ge3\). Explicit parameter regions with
\(2\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\) equilibria are described in \cite{GRPD2019}, and numerical evidence
for growth of the number of stable states with \(n\) goes back to
\cite{ThomsonGunawardena2009}. Thus for \(n\ge5\) there has been a gap between \(2n-1\) and the
largest number of equilibria known to occur, and for \(n\ge3\) a gap between the largest number
of stable equilibria known to occur for all \(n\), namely \(\lfloor n/2\rfloor+1\), and any
proved ceiling. No upper bound on the number of stable equilibria other than the trivial
\(2n-1\) appears to have been stated.

\subsection{Results}
For a rate vector \(\kappa\in\R^{6n}_{>0}\) and totals \(T=(\ET,\FT,\ST)\in\R^3_{>0}\) let
\(e(\kappa,T)\) be the number of positive equilibria of \eqref{eq:network} in the class
determined by \(T\); let \(h(\kappa,T)\) be the number of those at which the Jacobian of the
vector field, restricted to the stoichiometric subspace, is Hurwitz (all eigenvalues in the open
left half-plane); and let \(c(\kappa,T)\) be the number of those that are locally
asymptotically stable relative to the class. Clearly \(h\le c\le e\). We call
\[
 \capE=\max_{\kappa,T}e(\kappa,T),\qquad
 \capH=\max_{\kappa,T}h(\kappa,T),\qquad
 \capC=\max_{\kappa,T}c(\kappa,T)
\]
the \emph{equilibrium capacity}, the \emph{Hurwitz capacity} and the \emph{stable-state
capacity} of the \(n\)-site cycle. In each case the count is taken for one common rate vector
and one common triple of totals; equilibria of different systems or different classes are never
combined.

\begin{theorem}[Sharp capacities]\label{thm:main}
For every integer \(n\ge1\),
\[
 \capE=2n-1,\qquad \capH=\capC=n .
\]
More precisely:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[\textup{(a)}] For all positive rate constants and totals, the compatibility class
contains at most \(n\) locally asymptotically stable equilibria. Equilibria whose
linearization is singular or has imaginary eigenvalues are included in this count.
\item[\textup{(b)}] There are positive rational rate constants and totals for which the class
contains exactly \(2n-1\) equilibria, all positive, rational and hyperbolic; \(n\) of them are
sinks and the other \(n-1\) are saddles whose Jacobian has exactly one eigenvalue in the open
right half-plane.
\item[\textup{(c)}] The configuration in \textup{(b)} occurs on a nonempty open subset of
\(\R^{6n+3}_{>0}\), the space of all rate constants and totals.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The inequality \(\capE\le2n-1\) is the theorem of Wang and Sontag, and \(\capE=2n-1\) was known
for \(n\le4\) \cite{FHC2014}. For \(n=1\) the unique equilibrium is globally attracting
\cite{AngeliSontag2008}, and for \(n=2\) two stable equilibria occur \cite{HellRendall2015}; to
our knowledge, a proof that \(n\) stable equilibria occur is new for every \(n\ge3\), and so is
the ceiling (a) in the generality stated. Part (a) bounds the number of attracting \emph{equilibria}. It does
not bound the number of attractors of other kinds, such as periodic orbits, and the theorem
does not assert that every trajectory converges to an equilibrium.

The equilibria in (b) are explicit. Order them by the ratio \(u=E/F\) of free kinase to free
phosphatase and label them \(X_0,\dots,X_{2n-2}\). The construction realizes any prescribed
values of these ratios (Theorem~\ref{thm:count}), and in the stable realization the
even-indexed equilibria are the sinks. The latter are obtained by splitting a single
equilibrium of multiplicity \(2n-1\), and the splitting is governed by an exact law.

\begin{theorem}[Splitting law]\label{thm:split}
Let \(n\ge2\) and let \(\xi_0<\dots<\xi_{2n-2}\) be real numbers. In the family of systems
constructed in Section~\ref{sec:lift}, depending on a parameter \(\delta>0\) and having
equilibria \(X_0(\delta),\dots,X_{2n-2}(\delta)\) with \(\sqrt{1+8E/F}=3+\delta\xi_j\) at
\(X_j(\delta)\), the restricted Jacobian at \(X_j(\delta)\) has \(3n-1\) eigenvalues whose real
parts are negative and bounded away from zero, and one real eigenvalue
\begin{equation}\label{eq:splitlaw}
 \lambda_j(\delta)=-\,a\,\delta^{2n-2}\prod_{k\ne j}(\xi_j-\xi_k)\,\bigl(1+O(\delta)\bigr),
 \qquad \delta\to0,
\end{equation}
with a constant \(a>0\) that does not depend on \(j\) and is given by \eqref{eq:aconst}. In
particular \(\lambda_j<0\) for even \(j\) and \(\lambda_j>0\) for odd \(j\).
\end{theorem}

Within this family the separation of the equilibria in any smooth observable is of order
\(\delta\), while the slowest relaxation time is of order \(\delta^{-(2n-2)}\). Section~\ref{sec:consequences}
records this and further consequences of the proof: a closed formula for the stability margin of
the limiting transverse dynamics, which decays like \(n^2(2/9)^{n}\); an explicit diagonal
Lyapunov matrix, which makes the construction insensitive to the choice of the first \(n-1\)
catalytic currents; and the elementary corollary that a single cycle can hold \(q\) stable
equilibrium labels if and only if \(n\ge q\). Section~\ref{sec:examples} contains exact
rational witnesses of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b) for \(n\le7\) with small parameters, and a
three-site benchmark in declared physical units with a rigorous local certificate for
independent perturbations of all \(21\) parameters.

\subsection{Idea of the proof}
\emph{The ceiling.} The compatibility class is a compact convex polytope, the vector field
points inward or is tangent on its boundary, and there are no equilibria on the boundary. Hence
the Brouwer degree of the negative vector field on the interior of the class is \(+1\). When all
equilibria are nondegenerate this gives \(n_+-n_-=1\), where \(n_\pm\) are the numbers of
equilibria of index \(\pm1\), and with \(n_++n_-\le2n-1\) we get \(n_+\le n\). A sink has index
\(+1\). To include degenerate attractors and degenerate parameter values we use two classical
facts: an asymptotically stable equilibrium has index \(+1\) whether or not it is hyperbolic,
and, because the mass-action vector field depends linearly on the rate constants through a
matrix of full rank, equilibria are nondegenerate for a dense set of rate vectors.

\emph{The equilibria.} At a positive equilibrium all concentrations are monomials in
\(u=E/F\), \(s=S_0\) and \(f=F\), and the totals are
\(\ET=uf(1+sB(u))\), \(\FT=f(1+suD(u))\), \(\ST=sA(u)+usf(B(u)+D(u))\) for polynomials \(A,B,D\)
whose \(3n\) coefficients are arbitrary positive numbers determined by the rate constants. The
enzyme totals determine \(s\) and \(f\), and the equilibria are the solutions of one scalar
equation \(\Phi(u)=\ST\). On the locus \(A=(1+u)D\), \(\ST=\ET+\FT\), the numerator of
\(\Phi-\ST\) is a quadratic form whose discriminant is a square multiple of \(1+8u\); in the
variable \(x=\sqrt{1+8u}\) it factors, and the equation becomes the linear interpolation
condition \((x-1)(B-rD)=2(r-u)D\), \(r=\ET/\FT\). The even and odd parts of
\(\prod_j(x-x_j)\) solve this problem for \(r=\infty\) and are generated by a recurrence with
visibly positive coefficients; a B\'ezout-type quotient moves the solution to finite \(r\); and
positivity of the quotient follows because the two polynomials have real, negative, interlacing
roots.

\emph{Stability.} The \(3n\) rate constants that do not enter the equilibrium equations can be
chosen freely. We let all prescribed roots coalesce at \(x=3\) and use this freedom in three
steps (Figure~\ref{fig:flow}). First, binding and unbinding are made fast. The slow variables
are then the inventories \(\Lambda_i=S_i+C_{i+1}+Y_i\) of substrate with \(i\) phosphate groups,
\emph{including} the substrate bound to either enzyme; discarding the bound substrate would
change the slow dynamics. The linearized slow dynamics is \(\bK\,\diag(\bomega)\), where
\(\bomega\) is the vector of catalytic currents and
\(\bK=(-\bR+\one\bh^{T})\bM^{-1}\bR^{T}\) is formed from the path incidence matrix \(\bR\), a
positive definite ``mass matrix'' \(\bM\), and a feedback vector \(\bh\) that expresses
enzyme sequestration. Second, the current through the last site is made small. The spectrum
is then that of the leading \((n-1)\times(n-1)\) block of \(\bK\), plus a simple zero that is
forced by coalescence. Third, as the kinase excess \(r\) tends to infinity, \(r\) times this
block converges to \(\bB_*=-\bH+\one\bphi^{T}\), where \(\bH\) is the Dirichlet-type matrix of a
weighted path and \(\bphi\) is entrywise positive. This is a positive-feedback perturbation of a
stable matrix, and its stability is decided by the scalar gain \(G=\bphi^{T}\bH^{-1}\one\). The
moments of the coalesced polynomials give
\[
 1-G=\frac{v\,(50n-45)}{9\,(14-8v)}>0,\qquad v=(3n-2)\Bigl(\frac29\Bigr)^{n-1}.
\]
Finally the roots are split again. Every equilibrium inherits \(3n-1\) stable eigenvalues, and
the sign of the remaining real eigenvalue is read off from a determinant formula. The order of
the choices is \(n\), then \(r\), then the last current, then the binding time scale, then the
root separation; no estimate uniform in \(n\) is needed.

The general strategy of coalescing equilibria and then controlling the transverse spectrum by a
singular perturbation is that of \cite{FRW2020}; see also \cite{HellRendall2015}. What is new
here is an equilibrium geometry that reaches the maximal count, a reduction that keeps the
enzyme-bound substrate, and a transverse stability proof that works for every \(n\).

\subsection{Scope}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is a statement about the deterministic, well-mixed mass-action model
\eqref{eq:network} with irreversible catalytic steps. It counts stable equilibria. It is not a
statement about Shannon capacity, stochastic retention times, switching between states, or
inheritance through cell division, and the open set in (c) comes without an estimate of its
size. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} returns to these points.

\subsection{Formal verification and reproducibility}
The Lean~4 \cite{Lean4} development with Mathlib \cite{Mathlib} formalizes both universal
capacity ceilings, including nonhyperbolic attractors, simultaneous rational attainment with
the full sink/saddle spectrum, and open persistence of that same configuration. It also
formalizes the general prescribed-root construction, global conversion positivity,
interlacing and coefficient ratios, the common-witness quantitative splitting law and its
compact-current and closed-interval refinements, fixed rational spectra and the nominal
benchmark, and the same-parameter operational recovery and measured-readout guarantees.
Supporting proofs retain the original rational four-loop first-success procedure and the
analytical readout and reciprocal-time corollaries. Section~\ref{sec:formal} identifies the
exact statements, shared witnesses, and source-bound verification records. Supporting
proofs also construct embedded analytic unstable curves at the same rational family's
saddles. These curves and their backward trajectories remain in the positive compatibility
class and tend to their base equilibria backward in time. All numbers in the paper are
reproduced in exact rational arithmetic
by a script distributed with the source.
